Text analysis

Manuscript layouts and linguistic trends, measured from the TEI-XML encoding of every document in the archive.

    The corpus

    Where and when the documents in the archive were written.

    Eras and voices

    Which historical events the documents speak to, and whose voice they carry. Most surviving 19th-century records about displaced communities were written by outsiders, so the archive labels every document's perspective in its TEI header (<term type="perspective">).

    Manuscript layout

    How the pages are built: how densely they are written, in how many columns, on what material, and by how many hands. These come from <layout>, <supportDesc>, <handNote> and the <pb/>, <lb/> and <cb/> breaks in each transcription.

    Linguistic trends

    Which languages the documents are written in, how often writers switch between them, and how spelling and abbreviation practice changes. Words are credited to the language marked with xml:lang where they appear.

    How these figures are made

    • Every figure is computed from the TEI-XML files by tools/build_documents.py; nothing is entered by hand.
    • Words are counted from the normalised reading: expansions, corrections and regularisations count; abbreviations, original spellings, deleted text, notes, running heads and a translator's or editor's commentary do not. Chinese and Japanese characters count one word each.
    • Lines per page is the number of <lb/> elements divided by the number of <pb/> elements, so it measures what was transcribed, not the full physical page.
    • The most-frequent-word lists leave out common function words (for example "the", "and", "de", "yang") so that they show what the documents are about; all other counts include every word.
    • Rates per 1,000 words make long and short documents comparable. Periods with very few documents can swing widely, so check the document count beside each figure.
